TextBook Swap SA — Second-Hand Textbook Swap

PORTFOLIO DOCUMENT 04

1. Project Overview

Project Name: TextBook Swap SA

Project Type: Entrepreneurial Work-Integrated Learning (WIL) Project

Methodology: Agile Scrum

Project Focus: Student-to-student second-hand textbook marketplace

Current Progress: Sprints 1–6 completed; Sprint 7 outstanding.

TextBook Swap SA is an entrepreneurial project designed to help South African university students access affordable second-hand textbooks. The proposed marketplace aims to connect students who want to buy, sell, or exchange used textbooks, helping reduce the financial burden associated with purchasing academic materials.

The project follows an Agile approach, dividing the work into manageable sprints. Each sprint focuses on a particular stage of the project, from business planning and system design to development, testing, and final submission.

2. Purpose of the Sprint Backlog

The Sprint Backlog organises the tasks required to develop TextBook Swap SA. It provides a structured overview of the work planned for each sprint, the expected outcomes, and the criteria used to determine whether tasks meet their requirements.

The backlog supports planning, task prioritisation, progress monitoring, collaboration, and continuous improvement throughout the project.

4. Definition of Done

A task may be marked as completed when:

  • The required work has been carried out.
  • The result has been reviewed against the relevant requirements.
  • Any relevant defects or limitations have been recorded.
  • The task status reflects the actual work performed.
  • Appropriate documentation or other evidence is available where required.

A sprint-level status of completed does not automatically mean that every individual task has been independently verified.

5. Backlog Maintenance

The Sprint Backlog should be reviewed and updated as the project progresses. Task priorities and statuses should reflect the group's actual work. Unfinished activities should remain outstanding or be carried forward where appropriate.

The Product Backlog represents the overall list of project work, while the Sprint Backlog organises and tracks work associated with the individual sprints.

6. Overall Reflection

Completing Sprints 1–6 as a group allowed us to progress through the main stages of the TextBook Swap SA project, from business planning and system analysis to interface design, development, and testing. Dividing the project into sprints helped organise the work into manageable stages and provided a clearer understanding of how each stage contributed to the overall objectives.

Throughout the project, we developed a better understanding of Agile planning, task organisation, system requirements, user interface design, implementation, and quality assurance. The process also reinforced the importance of teamwork, communication, reviewing progress, and addressing challenges as they arise.

One of the key lessons learned was that successful project development requires both technical work and proper documentation. Planning and design provide direction, development turns requirements into functionality, and testing helps identify areas that need improvement.

Sprint 7 remains outstanding. The group's next priority is to complete the final documentation, review the system, prepare the presentation and demonstration, and submit the required deliverables. Completing these activities will help bring the project to a proper conclusion and ensure that the final portfolio accurately reflects the work completed.

The Sprint Backlog provides a structured overview of the seven stages of the TextBook Swap SA project. It connects business planning, system design, development, testing, and finalisation activities while supporting task prioritisation and progress monitoring.

The group has completed Sprints 1–6 and is preparing to complete Sprint 7. Keeping the backlog updated will help ensure that the outstanding tasks are managed effectively and that the final project documentation reflects the actual work completed.
